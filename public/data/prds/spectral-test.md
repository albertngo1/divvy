## Overview
A single-page browser instrument where the sound source is not an oscillator but a **pseudorandom number generator**, and the visual is the same stream plotted as consecutive triples in 3D. It's for programmers who've read "random numbers fall mainly in the planes" and never actually seen or heard it, and for people who just want a weird noise box with a real mechanism underneath.

## Problem
PRNG quality is taught as a table of test-suite pass/fail results. Nobody ever *perceives* a bad generator. But the flaws are wildly perceptible if you point the right sense at them: the low-order bits of a power-of-two LCG have periods of 2, 4, 8, 16 — that isn't noise, that's a **square wave at an exact subharmonic**. And consecutive triples from RANDU famously lie on 15 parallel planes, which is a shape, and shapes want to be rotated. The information is sitting there in every stdlib and it's rendered as nothing.

## How it works
- Pick a generator: RANDU, `glibc rand()`, a 16-bit LCG, xorshift32, PCG32, Middle-Square, Mersenne Twister. Set the seed.
- **The bit organ:** a rack of faders, one per output bit. Each fader routes bit *k* of the stream into the audio graph as a ±1 sample at the current sample rate. Bit 0 of a mod-2³¹ LCG has period 2 → you hear a pure square at Nyquist/2. Bit 3 → period 16. Bit 30 → hiss. Sweep from low bits to high and you hear a generator *become* random, one octave at a time. Same rack works as a rhythm machine at low rates: the binary period ladder is a free polyrhythm generator.
- **The lattice:** a WebGL point cloud of (x_n, x_{n+1}, x_{n+2}) normalized to a unit cube, orbit-controllable. A "find the planes" button eases the camera to the known critical viewing angle (for RANDU, look down the 9x − 6y + z direction) and the cloud collapses into stripes. A scored mini-mode: you're given an unlabeled generator and must rotate until the planes appear, and it grades you on how close you got to the true lattice normal.
- A pair of numbers always visible: measured spectral-test figure of merit in 2D/3D, and the audible "tonality" (spectral flatness of the current bit mix). Bad generators score low on both, and you can see the two agree.

## Technical approach
- Vanilla TS, no framework. Audio via an `AudioWorkletProcessor` that runs the generator step *inside the audio thread* — one LCG step is a multiply-add, trivially real-time at 48 kHz — and mixes selected bits with per-bit gains passed as processor params.
- Generators implemented on `Uint32Array` state with explicit `Math.imul` for 32-bit wraparound (JS doubles will silently lie to you here; this is the classic bug).
- Visuals: `regl` or raw WebGL2, a single VBO of ~200k triples updated in a ring buffer from a separate (non-audio) generator instance so audio and video don't fight over state.
- Spectral test: for the 3D case, compute the shortest nonzero vector of the dual lattice generated by {(1, a, a²)} mod m via a small Gaussian/LLL reduction on a 3×3 basis — that shortest vector *is* the plane normal, which is what the "find the planes" camera flies to. Doing this generically (rather than hardcoding RANDU's 9,−6,1) is the genuinely nice piece of math.
- **Hard part:** making the audio *honest*. It's easy to accidentally build a pretty noise toy where the sound isn't actually derived from the defect. The invariant to defend: a truly good generator must sound like flat white noise on every bit fader, and if it doesn't, the bug is mine.

## v1 scope
- Three generators: RANDU, xorshift32, PCG32.
- 32 bit faders, one master gain, one seed box.
- 3D point cloud with orbit control and a hardcoded "snap to RANDU planes" button.
- Spectral flatness readout.

## Out of scope
- Full TestU01/PractRand-style batteries.
- Recording, MIDI, presets, sequencing.
- Cryptographic generators (nothing to see — that's the joke, but it's not v1).

## Risks & unknowns
- Bit-level output on the audio thread at 48 kHz may just sound like a harsh buzz; the low-rate rhythm mode may end up being the actually-fun half.
- The LLL step on a 3×3 basis is small but fiddly to get right for arbitrary moduli.
- Audience is narrow — it lives or dies on one shareable 20-second video of the cloud collapsing into planes while the chord clarifies.

## Done means
With RANDU selected, the "find the planes" button reveals exactly 15 visually distinct hyperplanes, the same critical direction is derived at runtime by the lattice reduction rather than hardcoded, and soloing bit 0 of the 16-bit LCG produces a measurably periodic (spectral flatness < 0.1) tone while soloing bit 0 of PCG32 stays above 0.9.
