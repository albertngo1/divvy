## Overview
An inverse filter search for images. G'MIC ships 500+ filters with unbounded parameters; this points that library backwards — you give it a source and a target look, it returns a runnable `gmic` command line that reproduces the look, plus a similarity score. For photographers, pixel artists, and anyone who has ever said "what's your recipe."

## Problem
Every filter library is a one-way function. You can apply a look; you cannot ask *which* look was applied. So people reverse-engineer film sims, friends' edits, and a beloved 2013 Instagram feel by hand, sliding sliders for an hour and landing somewhere close-but-wrong. The search space is real, structured, and completely unsearched.

## How it works
**Stage 1 — fingerprint and shortlist.** Compute cheap descriptors on the target: per-channel histogram moments, LAB color-transfer statistics (Reinhard mean/std), gradient-magnitude spectrum, local-contrast profile, halo signature around strong edges (the unsharp tell), chroma-noise and grain power spectral density. Compare against a precomputed library: ~60 common G'MIC filters × sampled parameters × a fixed probe image set, stored as descriptor vectors. Nearest neighbours give a shortlist of ~8 plausible filters.
**Stage 2 — evolve the chain.** CMA-ES over chains of length 1–3 drawn from the shortlist, optimizing a weighted perceptual objective: histogram EMD + gradient-stat distance + LPIPS. Population ~40, ~60 generations, `gmic` invoked headlessly across worker processes.
**Output.** The literal command, a side-by-side, a confidence number, and a diverse top-5 rather than one over-confident answer. Mischief mode: **de-bake**, which searches for the chain that best undoes a look, so you can strip a filter off a JPEG someone posted.

## Technical approach
Python, `gmic-py` (fallback: `subprocess` to the `gmic` CLI), numpy/scikit-image, the `cma` package, ONNXRuntime for LPIPS. Data model: a filter spec table (name, param ranges, cost, whether it's local or global) and a cached descriptor library as parquet. The genuinely hard part is twofold: the space is discrete × continuous and non-differentiable, so gradient methods are out; and it's badly non-identifiable — several chains land within noise of each other. Mitigations: an MDL-style penalty on chain length, a prior pulling parameters toward filter defaults, and reporting diversity instead of pretending to a unique inverse.

## v1 scope
- 8 hand-picked filters, chain length exactly 1.
- Grid search over ≤3 parameters at 512px.
- Prints the best command and a before/after/recovered triptych PNG.

## Out of scope
Video, RAW pipelines, local/masked edits, dodge-and-burn, LUT export, GUI.

## Risks & unknowns
Many real looks are unreachable by any global filter chain — the tool must say "unreachable, best approximation is X" instead of confabulating. `gmic` throughput bounds the population size. Perceptual metrics regularly disagree with the human eye on color casts.

## Done means
On 20 held-out targets generated by random 2-filter chains, the search recovers the correct filter identities in ≥60% of cases and reaches median LPIPS < 0.05 against the target, in under 5 minutes per image on a laptop — with an honest "unreachable" verdict on a control set of hand-retouched images.
