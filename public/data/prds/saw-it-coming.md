## Overview

A 3–4 player, one-round game (~5 minutes) where the room writes a single sentence together, one word at a time, and each player is secretly trying to steer it toward a private target word. The catch: a small in-browser language model measures its own **entropy** after every word you add. If your word makes the model too *confident* about what comes next — if you've telegraphed where this is going — the word is rejected in front of everyone and you lose your turn. Legibility is the failure state.

## Problem

Hidden-intent games need a tell, and the tell is usually social — a hesitation, a glance. That's fine but it's the same tell in every game ever made. Here the tell is generated by a machine that has read a lot of English and is very good at noticing when a sentence has committed to a destination. You are not hiding from your friends. You are hiding from a next-token distribution, in public, in real time, and your friends get to watch its confidence spike.

## How it works

**Setup.** Host TV shows a seed stem: *"The party would have been fine if"*. Each phone privately receives a target word — `ostrich`, `divorce`, `linoleum`.

**Private probing (the reason you need your own phone).** At all times, every phone has a text field and a private **heat dial**. Type a candidate word and your phone scores the entropy of the model's next-token distribution for `sentence + candidate` — before committing. Green means the model is still lost; red means you've made the ending obvious. Everyone probes simultaneously, secretly, with different candidates in mind, and probes are never surfaced anywhere on the TV. A single passed-around phone destroys this game completely: the probing *is* the game, and it's private.

**Turns.** Strict rotation, one word each. On commit, the host recomputes entropy. Below the floor → **REJECTED** flashes red on the TV, the word is discarded and shown, and the turn passes. Above → the word joins the sentence and the TV's entropy gauge visibly moves. Ten words total, then everyone privately submits one guess at another player's target word.

**Scoring.** +3 if your target word lands in the sentence. +2 for correctly guessing a rival's target. −1 per rejection. The tension is exact: getting closer to your word is what makes you readable.

Private: target word, probes, heat dial, guesses. Shared: sentence, entropy gauge, rejections.

## Technical approach

Host tab runs distilgpt2 via transformers.js and holds the authoritative KV cache for the committed sentence; committing a word is one forward pass and a Shannon entropy over the resulting logits. Phones run the same model in a service worker via WebLLM for probing, so probe text never touches the wire; the fallback for weak phones is a debounced (250ms) `probe` message to the server with a per-player token bucket of 4/sec, answered on a private channel.

State in one Durable Object: `{stem, committed[], turnIdx, targets{playerId→word}, entropyHistory[], rejections[]}`. Sync is turn-based and authoritative — probes are read-only and never mutate room state, which keeps the fast path from ever racing the slow path.

The genuinely hard part is **calibrating the floor**. Absolute entropy swings wildly by position: after "the" the model is wide open, after a comma it's narrow, and after any period it collapses. A fixed threshold rejects innocent words and waves through blatant steering. The floor must be *relative*: sample the model's own top-8 plausible continuations at that slot, compute entropy for each, and reject your word only if it falls in the bottom quartile of that local distribution. Percentile-within-the-model's-own-options is the only version that feels fair, and getting this right is most of the build.

## v1 scope

- One round, one hardcoded stem, 3 players, exactly 10 words.
- Server-side probing only (no phone-local model); private probe channel.
- Entropy gauge is a single number and a bar on the TV.
- Three hardcoded target words. No guess phase if it isn't done by Friday.

## Out of scope

Phone-local WebLLM, multi-round, player-chosen stems, phrase-length plays, spectator mode, any anti-cheat beyond "don't show your phone."

## Risks & unknowns

distilgpt2 may be too dumb to notice steering at 10 words — the rejection may read as random, which would be fatal. Test with a planted transcript that obviously steers toward one word and confirm rejection fires. Second risk: probe latency over the wire making the dial feel dead; 250ms debounce plus optimistic dimming.

## Done means

Three phones join, each sees a different secret word, all three can probe candidates privately with a dial that responds in under 400ms, a deliberately on-the-nose steering word is rejected on the TV with the entropy value shown, an oblique one is accepted, and the sentence reaches ten words with a final score.
