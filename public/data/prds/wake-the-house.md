## Overview

A 3-player, one-round hidden-movement game for a living room with a TV and three phones. Everyone is a kid sneaking around the house after bedtime, each with a private list of errands. Two kids in the same room at the same time is the disaster — and the only person who knows *which* room creaked is the one who wasn't in it. For groups who like lying to each other with a straight face and no cards to hide behind.

## Problem

Hidden-movement games (Scotland Yard, Nocturne) hand one player omniscience and everyone else a map. The information is static — it belongs to a role. The itch: an information asymmetry that is *generated by the collision itself*, rotating unpredictably, so the person who is briefly omniscient changes every beat and has every incentive to spend that knowledge dishonestly.

## How it works

The TV shows a 4-room house — Kitchen, Bathroom, Hallway, Landing — with **no occupancy shown ever**, a Noise meter (0–3), and each player's *count* of completed errands.

Each phone privately holds an Errand List: 3 errands, each bound to a room and a dwell cost ("Glass of water — Kitchen ×2 beats", "Charger — Landing ×1"). Errands overlap rooms across players by design.

Six beats. Each beat:

1. **Select (12s):** every phone privately picks a room. Nothing is broadcast.
2. **Resolve:** any room holding ≥2 players collides. Both lose the beat and their in-progress dwell resets to zero. The TV plays one anonymous *creak* — no room, no names — and Noise +1.
3. **Intel:** exactly the players *not* in the collision get a private card naming the room and the number of bodies in it. With 3 players that is one person. If all three pile into one room, nobody gets a card and Noise +2.
4. **Talk (20s):** open table talk. Each phone may push at most one public **Claim** card to the TV ("I heard it in the Bathroom"), attributed by name, truth optional — so the innocent informer isn't automatically outed by speaking.

Scoring is individual: completed errands only. At Noise 3 the parents wake, the round ends instantly, and *in-progress errands are lost*. So whoever is ahead wants creaks, and will steer the other two into the same room on purpose.

The crux: a collider knows only their own room, so "was that us, or was that them in the Bathroom?" is genuinely open — while the innocent knows exactly and is selling a different answer.

## Technical approach

Host browser tab + phone PWA + PartyKit Durable Object per room code. Server-authoritative state machine: `LOBBY → SELECT(12s) → RESOLVE → TALK(20s) → …×6 → SCORE`.

Data model: `Room{code, beat, phase, noise, players[]}`, `Player{token, name, errands[{room, need, progress, done}], selection|null, intel[]}`. Selections are held server-side and never echoed; resolution is atomic at the deadline. Every outbound message is per-socket — the host socket gets a redacted view (noise, creak flag, completion counts, claims) and each phone gets only its own errands plus any intel card it earned.

Timing is trivially easy here (12-second beats). The genuinely hard part is **information containment**: no shared broadcast payload may ever contain the full occupancy map, so the host view and each phone view are separate projections of one authoritative state, and reconnects must re-hydrate private state from a player token without replaying anything a phone shouldn't have seen. Second-hardest: tuning the errand deck so room contention is high enough that collisions are likely but avoidable.

## v1 scope

- Exactly 3 players. No 4th, no spectators.
- 4 rooms, 6 beats, one round, one hardcoded 9-card errand deck.
- One creak sound, one noise meter, plain text on phones.
- 4-letter room code, no accounts, no reconnect polish.

## Out of scope

- 4–6 players (breaks the exactly-one-innocent math).
- Multiple rounds, persistent scores, house layouts, art, mobile animation.
- Voice, timers on the talk phase, claim validation.

## Risks & unknowns

- Do colliders infer too fast? If "my room + one creak" is usually solvable, the bluffing collapses; mitigate with a 4th room or one-per-beat "decoy creak."
- The leader's incentive to wake the house may make the endgame nihilistic rather than tense.
- 20s of talk may be too long with only three people and one claim card each.

## Done means

Three phones and a laptop complete one 6-beat round: a deliberate double-pick on the Kitchen produces a creak on the TV that names no room, a private card naming "Kitchen ×2" appears on exactly the third phone, that player publicly claims "Bathroom," and at least one playtester is visibly wrong about who collided at reveal.
