## Overview
Carrier Sense is a 3-player, no-talking agreement game. The TV displays eight icons; the room must all lock in the *same* icon. Each player is privately forbidden from two of them. The only communication channel in the room is a single lamp on the host screen, which lights whenever *any* phone's press pad is held — so nobody can tell who is transmitting, and two people pressing at once merge into one indistinguishable blink.

## Problem
Wordless-convergence games usually hand players a channel with built-in semantics (a heat map, a shared cursor, a tally). Carrier Sense hands them a channel with *no* semantics at all: one bit, no attribution, no addressing, collisions. The fun is watching a room spontaneously invent a protocol — long blink versus short, count-the-flashes, listen-before-transmit — and then watch it break the first time two people talk over each other. It's the social experience of building language under pressure, compressed into a minute and a half.

## How it works
PHONE (private): your two forbidden icons, a big press pad, and a lock-in selector over the eight icons. Nothing about anyone else. Every phone's veto set is different, and vetoes are dealt so exactly two icons are legal for all three — so agreement is possible but only after information moves.

HOST TV (shared): the eight numbered icons, one big lamp, a 90-second clock, and three lock indicators (locked / not locked, never *what* was locked). The lamp is the wired-OR of all press pads. Overlapping presses are one blink.

Phones are transmitters; the TV is the sole receiver. Everyone watches the same lamp and decodes it privately against their own vetoes. When all three have locked, the host reveals the three choices: win if identical and legal for everyone; otherwise it shows whose veto was violated, which is itself the joke.

## Technical approach
Authoritative WebSocket server (PartyKit / Socket.IO over Tailscale Serve). State: `room{phase, deadline, lampHolders:Set, locks:{pid:iconId}}`, `player{id, vetoes:[a,b], locked}`. Phones emit `press`/`release` with client timestamps; the server stamps authoritative times and broadcasts to the host only. Veto sets are generated by choosing the 2-icon legal intersection first, then dealing the remaining six as three disjoint pairs.

Hard part: perceptual fidelity of the lamp. Players will encode meaning in *durations and gaps*, so jitter is not cosmetic — it corrupts the language. Render lamp events through a fixed ~150ms display buffer so intervals replay in true relative timing rather than as-they-arrive, quantize nothing, and target sub-40ms error on blink length. Also verify no side channel leaks attribution (no per-player animation, no lamp color shift, no ordering tell in the DOM).

## v1 scope
- Exactly 3 players, 8 icons, 2 vetoes each, one guaranteed 2-icon legal intersection
- One 90-second round, single lamp, press pad, lock-in selector
- Host reveal screen: three picks side by side, win/fail, veto violations

## Out of scope
- Multiple rounds, scoring, 4+ players, any text or emoji channel, audio blinks, protocol hints or tutorials

## Risks & unknowns
- Rooms may deadlock and produce 90 seconds of nothing; a fallback "seed convention" card on the TV ("try: long = no") is the mitigation, held back until playtests say it's needed
- Collisions may be so frequent with three players that the channel is useless; may need a minimum-blink-length and a visible "channel busy" shimmer
- Skill gap: one player who knows Morse dominates and the others become spectators

## Done means
Three phones join, each sees a distinct private veto pair, holding any press pad lights the host lamp within ~150ms with faithful duration, simultaneous presses render as a single indistinguishable blink, and a round ends with a reveal that correctly declares a win only when all three locks are identical and legal for all three players.
