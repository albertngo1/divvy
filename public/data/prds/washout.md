## Overview

A small self-hosted web app that runs single-subject randomized trials on you. You name an intervention (magnesium before bed, no screens after 9pm, 20g protein at breakfast) and an outcome your wearable already measures. It builds the randomization schedule, tells you each day what to do, harvests the outcome automatically, and at the end reports an effect size with an honest p-value. For quantified-self people who have twenty years of data and zero causal conclusions.

## Problem

Self-experiments are almost always garbage, in specific fixable ways. "Coffee on odd days" confounds with day-of-week. Trying something for two weeks and comparing to "before" confounds with season, training block, and regression to the mean. Effects carry over into the washout you didn't take. You peek at the data daily and stop when it looks good, which inflates false positives enormously. And you self-report the outcome, which is the least reliable instrument you own. Every one of these is a solved problem in trial design; none of them are in any habit tracker.

## How it works

Setup asks four things: the two arms (A vs B, or on vs off), the outcome metric, expected carryover duration, and trial length. The engine emits a **blocked randomization schedule** — within each block of 4 days, both arms appear twice, in a random order, with washout days inserted after any switch and excluded from analysis. Blocks respect day-of-week balance so Mondays aren't all arm A.

Each morning at a set hour, ntfy pushes today's assignment: `Day 14 — arm B — open envelope #14`. That's the at-home blinding protocol: for pill-shaped interventions you prepare numbered opaque envelopes once, and only the app knows the mapping. You genuinely don't know what you took.

During the trial the dashboard shows compliance and days remaining — and nothing else. Results are sealed. On the final day it unseals: per-arm means, effect size with CI, and a p-value from a **Fisher randomization test** — re-run the actual assignment procedure 10,000 times over your observed outcomes and count how often chance beat your real result. No normality assumptions, and it's only valid because the app held the true schedule the whole time.

## Technical approach

Python + FastAPI + SQLite, one container. Schedule generation is a constrained shuffle: permute within blocks, reject sequences violating washout or day-of-week balance, seed stored and sealed. Outcomes ingest nightly from Apple HealthKit via the Health Auto Export app's REST push (HRV, resting HR, sleep stages, deep sleep minutes) and from Garmin Connect via the `garminconnect` library (Body Battery, sleep score, training readiness). Data model: `trial`, `assignment(day, arm, is_washout, revealed_at)`, `observation(day, metric, value, source)`.

Analysis: per-day outcome joined to non-washout assignments, effect = difference in means; the randomization distribution is generated by re-drawing valid schedules from the same constraint set — not naive label permutation, which would be wrong for a blocked design and is where most people get this subtly incorrect. Secondary readout: a lag-1 carryover check comparing post-switch days to steady-state days, to tell you whether your washout was long enough to trust the whole thing.

The hard part is statistical honesty at low n. Fourteen days of HRV against typical within-person variance detects only large effects; the app must state the minimum detectable effect *up front*, at setup, before you spend three weeks discovering it could never have found anything.

## v1 scope

- One active trial, two arms, one outcome metric
- Blocked schedule with fixed 1-day washout
- Health Auto Export ingest only
- ntfy morning push, sealed dashboard, unseal + randomization test at end

## Out of scope

Multi-arm, crossover across multiple outcomes, Bayesian sequential stopping, sharing/social, iOS app.

## Risks & unknowns

Most realistic personal interventions have effects far below the noise floor of wearable HRV — the honest answer will usually be "inconclusive," which is correct and unsatisfying. Compliance decays. Blinding only works for a narrow class of interventions. Garmin's API is unofficial and breaks.

## Done means

A 28-day two-arm trial runs end to end: schedule generated and sealed, ≥90% of days auto-populated with a HealthKit outcome and zero manual entry, and the final report shows an effect size, CI, randomization-test p-value, and the minimum detectable effect stated at setup — with a positive-control trial (arm B = a hard evening workout) correctly detecting the known resting-HR bump.
