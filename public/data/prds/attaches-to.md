## Overview

A desktop/browser editor sidebar for people who write text that gets argued about later — contracts, RFCs, API specs, policy documents, regulatory filings. It doesn't check grammar or style. It checks whether your sentence has more than one *structure*, and whether those structures mean different things.

## Problem

The expensive ambiguities in a spec are almost never vocabulary. They're attachment and scope: "a written notice of termination or breach" (does *written* reach *breach*?), "users may only export data they own" (only-what?), "each customer with an active plan and an admin" (one entity or two?), "within 30 days of delivery or inspection". Grammar checkers are blind to all of it because both readings are perfectly grammatical. Writers are blind too, because they can only see the reading they meant.

## How it works

As you type, sentences with trigger constructions get a quiet underline. Click it and you get a card: **Reading A** and **Reading B**, each rendered as an unambiguous paraphrase with the differing span bracketed, plus a plain-English gloss of the difference ("A: the notice must be written. B: both the notice and the breach must be written."). Pick one and it applies a rewrite that forecloses the other — inserting *each of*, repeating the head noun, moving *only* next to what it modifies, splitting into an enumerated list.

Crucially it stays quiet when readings differ structurally but not meaningfully. That restraint is the product.

## Technical approach

TypeScript front end, Python service (or a WASM build for local-only use — the target users will not paste contracts into a cloud API, so local-first is a requirement, not a nicety).

Parse with `spaCy` + `benepar` for a constituency tree. Rather than enumerating the full parse forest (exponential and mostly junk), detect a fixed catalog of ~10 ambiguity schemas by pattern-matching over the dependency and constituency structure:

- PP attachment to competing heads within a window
- coordination scope with a shared pre-modifier (`ADJ (N and N)` vs `(ADJ N) and N`)
- relative-clause attachment across a coordination or possessive chain
- scope of `only` / `not` / `all` / `at least one` over a following coordination
- distributivity: `each`/`any` over a coordinated object
- `X or Y` under negation or under a temporal preposition

Each schema owns a paraphrase template, so Reading A/B are generated by deterministic tree surgery, not by a model — no hallucinated readings. A small logistic classifier over corpus features (do the two attachment heads take different argument distributions? do the coordinated nouns share a semantic type via embedding cosine?) suppresses the boring cases.

The hard part is precision. A linter that fires on every third sentence is uninstalled by lunch; the target is under one flag per 200 words on clean prose.

## v1 scope

- Three schemas only: shared pre-modifier over coordination, `only`-scope, PP attachment
- Paste-a-paragraph web page, no editor integration
- Two readings max per flag, with one-click rewrite
- Runs entirely local

## Out of scope

Anaphora/pronoun ambiguity, cross-sentence reference, defined-term consistency checking, VS Code/Word plugins, multi-language.

## Risks & unknowns

Parser errors on legal register (long sentences, `hereunder`, nested numbering) will produce phantom ambiguities; may need a numbering-aware pre-segmenter. Unclear whether users want detection or just the rewrite.

## Done means

On a hand-labeled set of 100 sentences drawn from real contracts (50 known-ambiguous, 50 clean), it hits ≥70% recall on the three v1 schemas at ≥85% precision, and its rewrites are judged to remove the ambiguity by a reader who never saw the original.
